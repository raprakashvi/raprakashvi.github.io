Which objects each view covers

How a record gets its tags

The three complete catalogues are in the same collection as the two releases, with the same filters and the same views. Since 2026-11-16 the sort is not a three-way partition but two overlapping tags — a record may carry neither, one or both (scripts/web/unified/catalogue.py):

  1. Medicine

    The museum’s own words name a medical or surgical instrument: a Boerhaave classification under 10 Geneeskunde, hygiene, farmacie (packaging excluded), a NEMO medical-technology category, or a term from a stated word list matched on whole words.

  2. Science and technology

    The T-030 codebook labelled the record with a named principle of physics, chemistry, life science or earth science.

  3. Paper media are not science

    A print, drawing, photograph, lantern slide, negative, letter or book gets no Science tag from a principle that a label on its medium produced. Every flat or paper record is matched on whole words, not substrings, so diagnostiek is not a slide and fotometer is not a photograph.

  4. Neither

    Everything else. The record stays in the collection under All, is counted on the Data page, and is never dropped.

Life of a principle: one list, every source

Principles and working functions share one chip list. Every lane draws objects from the surgical release, the science release and the three catalogues, coloured by museum. Surgical instruments have no codebook principle of their own; they join the principle the codebook most often gives to the museum records that carry both the Medicine tag and a principle — computed from the data by the build, never chosen by hand — and sit in their own lane, Medical practice. The mapping is written in scripts/web/unified/build.py and printed here.

The map and the groups

How the map is drawn, and how its settings were chosen

The map follows standard practice for dimensionality reduction, and the settings were picked by measurement, not by look. The vectors are L2-normalised, reduced to 50 principal components, and laid out with UMAP on those components (cosine metric, spectral initialisation, fixed seed). A random initialisation is treated as an error: if UMAP cannot initialise spectrally it is re-initialised from the principal components (deterministic), and if that also fails the build fits a seeded subsample and places the rest with the fitted reducer — never a silent random start. The quality of each candidate layout is measured against the 50-dimensional space the layout is computed from: trustworthiness (few false neighbours appear close), continuity (few true neighbours are torn apart) and k-NN preservation for k = 10. The same numbers are given for two baselines, PCA to two dimensions and t-SNE. The default is the UMAP setting with the highest k-NN preservation; ties go to trustworthiness, then continuity, then the smaller neighbourhood.

A sub-collection (a tag, a museum, a principle, a device family or a century) is laid out on its own on the same pipeline and gets its own cached layout and its own measured metrics; the map's selector shows them. The two-dimensional layout is for viewing only: the groups by closeness are always found in the full high-dimensional space, never in the plane, and for that reason a sub-collection view does not outline groups.

Dates: a catalogue record whose own date falls outside 1450–2035 is treated as undated on the charts and in the group lines (the same stated rule the T-030 analyses use); the two curated releases keep their own checked dates, including Roman instruments.

The space is the frozen SigLIP 2 space: the photograph and the title are embedded by SigLIP 2 ViT-B/16 (photograph) and its text tower (title), museum style is removed within each tag group, and the two cosines are z-scored and summed. Nothing on this site uses a supervised head on those vectors: the catalogue head of T-029 did not transfer to the platform, and a learned space is offered only if it beats the frozen space on every benchmark (T-035).
