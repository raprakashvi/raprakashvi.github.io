Work sequence

    The full plan, claim gates and operational protocol are in RESEARCH_PLAN.md, CONTRIBUTIONS.md and STUDY_PROTOCOL.md alongside the atlas. Prior planning documents are preserved in archive/phase1-2026-10-06/.

    Sources & images

    Institutional object records, an excavation report, manuscript catalogs and specialist history. Access dates and reading limits are recorded per source.

    Research notes

    This is a seed relationship graph, not a claim that all modern tools descend directly from Rome. Roman practitioners inherited earlier traditions, and later societies could retain, adapt or independently recreate forms. Multiple parents and convergent designs belong in the next iterations.

    What survived?

    Two meanings are kept separate: physical archaeological objects, and later tools that perform comparable tasks. Artifact survival establishes presence at a time and place. Resemblance establishes a comparison, not transmission.

    Where to start

    Stanway is the earliest securely contextualized kit in this seed, around 40–50/55 CE. It is not the earliest surgical toolkit worldwide. Broad catalog ranges extending into BCE cannot be ranked as exact first appearances. Earlier individual finds remain a research queue.

    Dates and missing evidence

    Negative years mean BCE, positive years CE; there is no year zero. Production, burial, site context, manuscript dates and discovery dates are different fields. Roman types without a dated original have null dates and appear in an undated section; they have no timeline bar. Alternate catalog dates and exact patent filing/publication dates appear in the detail panel. Manufacture ranges are not the first or last years a tool design was used.

    Reading the map

    Dated sequences group specific tool types and display each witness’s date, basis and distinguishing features. Their order is not proof of ancestry; overlapping ranges cannot establish priority. Family tree mode connects actual recorded comparisons in branching layouts. Dotted links from dark tool-type labels are organizational groupings, not ancestry. Dashed links are functional comparisons; extra links preserve multiple comparisons for a tool. Horizontal distance is relationship depth, not calendar time. Use the branch selector, drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and hover a node or link for sources. Left-clicking pins full details and selects the record's line: elders upstream and descendants downstream along the drawn links, with everything else faded. Show only this line keeps just that line in every view until Show all records. Right-click anywhere on the canvas, press Escape, or use Deselect lineage to clear the selection and restore all records if the line was isolated. Select lineage selects the current record again. Closing details keeps the lineage selected. Plus/minus, arrow keys and 0 (fit branch) also work when the tree has keyboard focus. Timeline mode uses linear calendar time and shows each date interval. Dashed edges mean functional comparison. Solid edges occur only in the separate book-transmission branch. Click a card for source references and unresolved evidence.

    Testing acceleration / deceleration

    First expand the corpus with dated objects, text illustrations, geographic scope and acquisition context. Define changes in geometry, material, mechanism and clinical task before counting innovations. Use regional and instrument-family comparisons, interval-censored dates, and sensitivity analyses for unidentified objects, replicas and disputed provenance.

    Raw find counts are not innovation rates. Exceptional burial, corrosion, recycling, excavation intensity and publication availability affect apparent gaps. A useful baseline is each region’s pre-event trend and contemporary regions or families with different exposure. This curated, purposive sample cannot yet support a causal estimate.

    Next evidence targets

    Earlier individual Hellenistic / Republican objects; Byzantine surgical texts and objects; additional instrument-level passages in Paul of Aegina and Al-Zahrawi; medieval Latin illustrations; Renaissance makers; surgical adoption of stainless steel; modern manufacturer catalogs. Record chapter / folio and object accession for every new link.

    Pictures

    Photographs, early printed illustrations and historical object plates have individual credits and reuse licenses. Reference plates are labeled when they show different specimens. Manuscript-copy, edition and photographic-reproduction dates are recorded separately from composition and burial dates. UVA images are permission-restricted and remain linked at their source. No generated image is used as archaeological evidence.
