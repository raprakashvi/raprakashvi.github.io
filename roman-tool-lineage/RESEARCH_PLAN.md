# Surgical instruments across knowledge boundaries

Phase II · exploratory pilot v0.3 · 6 October 2026

**Question:** What persists, what changes, and what crosses knowledge boundaries in surgical instruments? The Roman corpus is our reference panel. It is not a presumed root for every later tool.

**Proposed explanation to test:** task-specific mechanical arrangements may persist while component materials, attachment interfaces and maintenance practices change. Transmission, independent solutions to the same anatomical problem, and selective survival of evidence are competing explanations. A negative or mixed result is acceptable.

This is an exploratory plan, not a preregistration or a completed systematic survey. Exact proposed contributions and their acceptance gates are in [CONTRIBUTIONS.md](CONTRIBUTIONS.md); operational methods are in [STUDY_PROTOCOL.md](STUDY_PROTOCOL.md). The PNAS ambition calls for a generalizable empirical finding. Neither a larger gallery nor an attractive genealogy alone supplies that finding.

## Implemented first pass

[The pilot findings](PILOT_FINDINGS.md) now document 15 source readings, seven evidence contrasts, seven inspected documentary images, and a complete screen of the 16 named entries on Weiss 1863 p. 19. The atlas’s **Findings & boundaries** tab exposes these readings, their sources and the inclusion decisions. This is a completed exploratory pass, not completion of the independent-validation or historical-inference gates below.

The scientific contribution is to discover and test **which knowledge must accompany a design for it to be made, judged, fitted, used or replaced**. Translation ambiguity and catalog classification are boundaries in our evidence; manufacturing, quality testing and compatibility are candidate boundaries between historical activities. They must not be conflated. A historical boundary requires evidence of differential access, a transfer, or a failure—not merely a missing field in our corpus.

The first readings revise the working model: materials coexist, named tools conceal different component functions, and visible form underdescribes production and supply. We will test these post-reading hypotheses against matched source series and contrary cases. AI supports acquisition and source checking; using it is not an empirical contribution by itself.

The initial deep comparison will use **soft-tissue cutting** and **urinary access/drainage**. They offer contrasting component and mechanism histories. Grasping/clamping remains an extension until vessel occlusion, tissue grasping and bone cutting are separated into comparable tasks. Cautery, needling and cranial opening serve the cross-tradition scoping panel; they do not enter the same pooled outcome merely because all are medical.

## 2. Knowledge boundaries to investigate

Treat “civilizations” as starting search labels. Store specific place, institution, language, practitioner/workshop and date. Languages, states, religions and material traditions overlap; none is a homogeneous or timeless analytical unit. Museum location is not place of manufacture or use.

## 3. What would count as confluence?

Keep four propositions separate:

1. **Similarity:** two witnesses share explicitly coded traits within a comparable task. Record the alternatives and how much is unobserved.
2. **Opportunity for contact:** a dated translation, person, workshop, trade or teaching route existed. Broad geographic contact alone is weak evidence for a specific instrument.
3. **Transmission:** a source explicitly attributes a design or a distinctive correspondence can be linked through a dated channel. Preserve whether the claim concerns text, image, manufacture or use.
4. **Recombination/confluence:** identifiable features from distinct documented traditions occur together in a later design, with evidence supporting the proposed channels. A mixed-metal tool alone is not cultural confluence.

For each proposed connection, maintain a dossier: source and target identities; date roles; feature correspondence; channel evidence; independent attestation; copied-figure dependence; competing explanations; and an adjudicated claim type. Do not collapse these into an arbitrary confidence score. Unresolved cases remain unresolved, and missing links remain visible.

Start with the Arabic–Latin dossier because its translation attribution and glossary offer a concrete audit trail. The Greek-to-Arabic medical translation context is supported by [NLM’s overview](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/hmd/arabic/med_islam.html), but it is not an instrument-level route already established by this project. Ottoman adaptation and South/East Asian connections remain comparison questions.

## 4. Acquisition and analysis sequence

| Stage | Work | Required output / gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scope and novelty | Map existing instrument histories, craft/technical-knowledge studies and cultural-evolution methods; secure historian, language and material expertise; name complete source series | Frozen tasks, regions, series, inclusion rules and novelty matrix; no “first” claim without this review |
| 2. Coding feasibility | Independently double-code a stratified pilot; reconcile objects and copied texts; record original terms and date roles | Revised codebook, agreement/error report, identifiable contrasts, pre-specified held-out split |
| 3. Systematic acquisition | Run recorded catalog/bibliographic searches, screen complete eligible series, save negative and inaccessible results; acquire images after eligibility | Versioned source/witness/object/component/claim tables; no invisible exclusions |
| 4. Validate acquisition | Independent reference coding, original-language adjudication and source-dependence checks; add a benchmark only if claiming AI accuracy or efficiency | Measured field/date/citation errors and corrected corpus; no automation-success claim without a baseline |
| 5. Historical tests | Evaluate component-change contrasts and transmission dossiers; sensitivity to date widths, restoration, copied images and source selection | Effect estimates or bounds, competing explanations, and an explicit identifiability decision |
| 6. Replication and paper | Evaluate an independent series/region; reconcile manuscript narrative with results; prepare reproducible release | A general claim supported by replication, or a narrower documented conclusion with null results |

These are dependency gates rather than promised calendar dates. Set staffing and milestone dates once collaborator and archive access are known. A larger convenience sample cannot substitute for a failed gate.

## 5. Proposed primary analysis

Within each preselected source series and comparable task, identify successive **observed configurations** only when chronology and variant identity are sufficiently resolved. For each eligible pair, code whether material state changes and whether mechanical configuration changes. Estimate their joint distribution and the contrast between their change probabilities, with a shared denominator of pairs where both dimensions are observable. Report family/series-specific results before pooling.

This is a contrast in observed changes, not an invention rate, adoption rate or a genealogy. Define a balanced trait dictionary before measurement: an over-detailed material vocabulary paired with one coarse mechanism label would predetermine the result. Report per-character contrasts and sensitivity to category granularity, observation spacing and alternate date orderings. Use bounds where ordering is unresolved, and source/object clusters for uncertainty. Pilot simulations must determine whether the intended distinction is estimable.

For transmission, evaluate dossiers separately from similarity clustering. A similarity network may nominate comparisons. It cannot supply the independent channel evidence used to validate itself. Distinguish inherited text/illustrations from separately attested manufacture or clinical use. Historical-event models are deferred until exposure and comparison series are defensible.

## 6. Contemporary and image work

The existing chronology still ends in 1980. To reach the contemporary endpoint, sample dated manufacturer catalog series and IFUs, including handle/blade interfaces, polymers, single-use assemblies, sterilizable components, endoscopic shafts and powered actuators. Verify introduction and adoption separately from the date a product webpage was observed. Archive catalog versions and identify model numbers.

This pass adds 14 images to existing later records: twelve museum photographs and two original patent drawing sheets. They improve inspection, not temporal completeness. Four later records still lack matched images; the tab and `results/tables/image_coverage.csv` expose each gap. Images retain accession, role, source, rights and hash. A visually similar specimen is not silently substituted for the object described. Restricted or unresolved manuscript images remain source links.

## 7. Paper outline and stopping rules

Provisional title: **Persistence and recombination in surgical technologies across knowledge boundaries**. Keep this provisional until the empirical result exists.

Planned figures: sampling/dependency map; component/trait codebook with exact specimens; validated coverage and uncertainty; component-change contrasts; evidence of which knowledge accompanied transfer; one independently supported transmission dossier. Acquisition validation belongs in the methods; an AI comparison is optional unless an AI-benefit claim is made. Proposed broad contribution: how technical constraints, material choices and knowledge movement interact in durable technologies. The measured direction of that interaction is not predetermined.

If sampling remains opportunistic, publish the atlas as infrastructure without rate claims. If channels cannot distinguish transfer from convergence, report comparison dossiers without ancestry. If an AI-benefit comparison is undertaken and finds no reliable accuracy or time benefit, report that result. Such a comparison is not a required scientific contribution. If only one narrow series supports inference, scope the paper to that series. PNAS fit must be reassessed after the result and novelty review, not inferred from the visualization.
